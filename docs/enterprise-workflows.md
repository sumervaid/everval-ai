# Working as an enterprise team

Everval AI currently serves one trusted local workspace. Multiple organizational responsibilities can share its process, but reviewer names and operating-system labels are not authenticated roles.

## Engineering owner

Connect an authorized repository and inspect discovered candidates. Favor independently described changes with a stable expected behavior. Reject tasks whose setup, lineage, or scope cannot be reconstructed. Keep a record of excluded work; a high score on an unusually easy subset is not evidence of broad engineering capability.

## Evaluation engineer

Review issue statements without copying implementation details into solver context. Prepare and pin the environment, validate candidates repeatedly, and publish an immutable release. Use the development partition to refine prompts and harnesses. Reserve the holdout partition for decisions; the application exposes explicit partitions but cannot prevent an owner from contaminating one outside the system.

## Platform owner

Configure model endpoints, credential variable names, prices, harness limits, and per-run budgets. Verify runtime health. Schedules reuse frozen snapshots and budgets; they do not impose a lifetime spending cap.

## Reviewer and decision owner

Inspect failed and incomplete attempts. Add dimension scores and rationale alongside automated evidence. Use a versioned policy to express acceptable quality, coverage, cost, latency, and regression tolerance. A policy decision with insufficient evidence is inconclusive.

Export an evidence report for review, keeping private graders and source material within the permitted audience. Audit labels support local accountability; stronger organizational identity and external audit retention require additional deployment controls.
